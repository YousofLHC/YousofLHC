The Kalman Filter from First Principles

Study notes deriving the Kalman filter from the linear-Gaussian state-space model — prediction, update, information form, and its connection to message passing. Includes a printable PDF handout.

Download PDF handout

Study method

Read once for the "why", then re-derive each equation by hand on paper. The PDF handout has the compressed version for review.

1 · The linear-Gaussian state-space model

The filter assumes a process evolving linearly with Gaussian noise:

xt=Axt−1+wt,wt∼N(0,Q)x_{t} = A x_{t-1} + w_t, \qquad w_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q)xt​=Axt−1​+wt​,wt​∼N(0,Q)

with observations

zt=Hxt+vt,vt∼N(0,R).z_t = H x_t + v_t, \qquad v_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R).zt​=Hxt​+vt​,vt​∼N(0,R).

The goal: maintain the filtering distribution p(xt∣z1:t)p(x_t \mid z_{1:t})p(xt​∣z1:t​), which turns out to stay Gaussian forever, so it's fully described by a mean and covariance.

2 · Prediction (prior) step

Given p(xt−1∣z1:t−1)=N(x^t−1,Pt−1)p(x_{t-1} \mid z_{1:t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\hat{x}_{t-1}, P_{t-1})p(xt−1​∣z1:t−1​)=N(x^t−1​,Pt−1​):

x^t∣t−1=Ax^t−1\hat{x}_{t|t-1} = A \hat{x}_{t-1}x^t∣t−1​=Ax^t−1​ Pt∣t−1=APt−1A⊤+QP_{t|t-1} = A P_{t-1} A^\top + QPt∣t−1​=APt−1​A⊤+Q

Proof sketch: mean of an affine transform, plus the added process noise covariance.

3 · Update (correction) step

The innovation carries the new information:

y~t=zt−Hx^t∣t−1\tilde{y}_t = z_t - H \hat{x}_{t|t-1}y~​t​=zt​−Hx^t∣t−1​

The Kalman gain weighs prior vs measurement by their relative covariance:

Kt=Pt∣t−1H⊤(HPt∣t−1H⊤+R)−1K_t = P_{t|t-1} H^\top \big(H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R\big)^{-1}Kt​=Pt∣t−1​H⊤(HPt∣t−1​H⊤+R)−1

Posterior mean and covariance:

x^t=x^t∣t−1+Kty~t,Pt=(I−KtH)Pt∣t−1\hat{x}_t = \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + K_t \tilde{y}_t, \qquad P_t = (I - K_t H) P_{t|t-1}x^t​=x^t∣t−1​+Kt​y~​t​,Pt​=(I−Kt​H)Pt∣t−1​

Derive the gain yourself

Minimize E∥xt−x^t∥2\mathbb{E}\|x_t - \hat{x}_t\|^2E∥xt​−x^t​∥2. Differentiating w.r.t. KtK_tKt​ and setting to zero gives exactly the formula above — the estimator is simultaneously the MMSE, MAP, and conditional mean because everything is Gaussian.

4 · The information form (why it matters for message passing)

Inverting the update gives the information form of the filter — this is the form that makes Kalman filtering look identical to belief propagation:

Pt−1=Pt∣t−1−1+H⊤R−1H,Pt−1x^t=Pt∣t−1−1x^t∣t−1+H⊤R−1zt.P_t^{-1} = P_{t|t-1}^{-1} + H^\top R^{-1} H, \qquad P_t^{-1} \hat{x}_t = P_{t|t-1}^{-1} \hat{x}_{t|t-1} + H^\top R^{-1} z_t.Pt−1​=Pt∣t−1−1​+H⊤R−1H,Pt−1​x^t​=Pt∣t−1−1​x^t∣t−1​+H⊤R−1zt​.

These are exactly the sum-product rules from the factor graphs article: precision adds, and the information vector accumulates measurements. A Kalman filter is Gaussian belief propagation on a chain.
