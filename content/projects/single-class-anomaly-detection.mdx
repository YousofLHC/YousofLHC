## The problem

Anomalies are patterns in data that do not conform to a well-defined notion of
normal behavior — and they often carry critical information. Real-world anomaly
detection is complicated by **imbalanced data**, where the minority (anomalous)
class is poorly sampled, making conventional binary or multiclass classifiers
biased toward the majority class.

A natural remedy is **one-class classification (OCC)**: model only the normal
region, and declare any observation outside it an anomaly. Classic geometric
approaches based on convex hulls have high computational complexity and do not
scale, while most OCC methods reduce to costly quadratic programs.

## The proposed framework

The thesis proposes a novel one-class framework with three main ingredients:

1. **Implicit convex hull via elastic net.** Instead of computing the convex hull
   of the normal data explicitly, the decision boundary is estimated *implicitly*
   through elastic-net regularization. The combination of $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$
   terms yields both *sparsity* (feature selection) and *numerical stability*
   with a *grouping effect* among correlated features.
2. **Ensemble learning.** To improve accuracy and reduce the bias caused by data
   imbalance, sampling and random subspaces are combined into an ensemble of
   one-class models.
3. **Message-passing solvers.** The optimization is solved with proximal gradient
   methods and **Approximate Message Passing (AMP)**. To overcome AMP's
   convergence limitations under noisy, ill-conditioned matrices, its
   **Kalman-based variant (KAMP)** is used — merging Kalman-filter ideas with AMP
   for more stable and faster convergence. A distributed version, **DKAMP**,
   splits the measurement matrix across network nodes and exchanges small
   messages over diverse graph topologies for scalability.

## Experiments & results

The framework was evaluated on diverse benchmark datasets (including the ODDS
collection and CIFAR-10 boundary experiments) and compared with prominent
methods — **OCSVM, SVDD, LOF, IF, and EE**:

- Consistent improvement in arithmetic and geometric mean scores, precision,
  accuracy, recall, F1, and kappa.
- Statistically significant superiority of **KAMP over AMP** (Mann-Whitney U /
  Wilcoxon tests, p < 0.001) on Gaussian, heavy-tailed, and orthogonal random
  matrices, with lower error variance.
- Ability to form fully flexible decision boundaries in the input space and to
  construct convex hulls in high-dimensional feature space using polynomial
  kernels.

The combination of the implicit convex hull, elastic-net regularization,
AMP/KAMP/DKAMP, and ensemble learning delivers a coherent, scalable, and robust
framework for anomaly detection in real-world and complex datasets.
