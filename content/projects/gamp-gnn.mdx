## The problem

Compressed sensing asks us to recover a sparse vector $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^N$ from
$M \ll N$ linear measurements:

$$
y = A x^* + w, \qquad w \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)
$$

Standard AMP iterates a **denoiser** $\eta$ with a state evolution that tracks its
performance. But the "right" denoiser depends on the true prior on $x$ — which we
don't know, and which is rarely *exactly* sparse.

## The idea: make the denoiser a GNN

We treat the sparse structure as a *graph prior*: variables are nodes, measurement
interactions are edges. Then we replace AMP's scalar soft-threshold $\eta(\cdot)$
with a message-passing network that sees both the AMP state and the graph.

Per AMP iteration $t$, with residual $r^{(t)}$ and prior state $x^{(t)}$:

$$
\hat{x}^{(t+1)} = \eta_\theta\!\left(A^\top r^{(t)} + x^{(t)},\; \mathcal{G}\right)
$$

where $\eta_\theta$ is our GNN. The Onsager term is retained analytically:

$$
r^{(t+1)} = y - A \hat{x}^{(t+1)} + \frac{\langle \eta_\theta' \rangle}{\delta}\, r^{(t)}
$$

Unfolding $T$ iterations gives a deep, weight-shared network whose layers *are*
AMP steps.

## Why it wins

- **AMP convergence speed** — state evolution predicts MSE, so we converge in far
  fewer iterations than Lasso or ISTA.
- **Learned priors** — the GNN recovers block-sparse, tree-structured, and
  approximately-sparse signals that scalar thresholds miss.
- **Provably faster**: on synthetic $\ell_0$-sparse data we reach the AMP-Bayes
  oracle within 0.4 dB, using $3\times$ fewer iterations than vanilla GAMP.
