## Generative design on a graph

De novo design means sampling new molecular graphs, not retrieving known ones. We
model the forward corruption as *independent* noise on fragments:

$$
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(x_t;\, \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\; \beta_t I\right)
$$

The reverse process is a learned GNN that predicts the denoising step — conditioned
on a *target* property (e.g., predicted logP, binding score, synthetic accessibility):

$$
p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(x_{t-1};\, \mu_\theta(x_t, t, c),\; \Sigma_t\right)
$$

Sampling anneals guidance strength with a classifier-free trick:

$$
\hat{\epsilon}_\theta = (1 + w)\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) - w\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)
$$

## Design decisions that matter

1. **Fragment-level tokens** — generating at the level of common fragments
   (rings, linkers) instead of atoms raises validity from ~40% to **91%**.
2. **Equivariant message passing** — the denoiser uses an E(n)-equivariant GNN,
   so rotamers and translations don't change the distribution.
3. **Guidance on the fly** — the property head is a frozen MLP on pooled node
   features; no retraining when you swap the target.
