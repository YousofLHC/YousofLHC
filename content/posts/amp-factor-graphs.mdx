## The one idea behind everything

Every probabilistic model you will ever build can be drawn as a **factor graph**:
a bipartite graph where round nodes are *variables* $x_1, \dots, x_N$ and square nodes
are *factors* $f_a$ that couple them. The joint distribution factorizes as

$$
p(x_1, \dots, x_N) = \frac{1}{Z} \prod_{a} f_a\big(\{x\}_a\big).
$$

We want marginals $p(x_i)$ — and the most efficient way to get them is to pass
*messages* along the edges until the graph "agrees" with itself.

### The sum–product message

Define a message from factor $f$ to variable $x$ as

$$
\mu_{f \to x}(x) \;=\; \int f(x, x') \; \mu_{x' \to f}(x') \; dx'.
$$

and from variable to factor as a product of incoming messages:

$$
\mu_{x \to f}(x) \;=\; \prod_{g \in \mathcal{N}(x) \setminus f} \mu_{g \to x}(x).
$$

After iterating to convergence, the *belief* at a variable is

$$
b(x) \;\propto\; \prod_{f \in \mathcal{N}(x)} \mu_{f \to x}(x).
$$

On a tree these beliefs are *exactly* the marginals. On loopy graphs, they are an
approximation — usually a shockingly good one.

## The linear-Gaussian special case

Let the model be $x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, P_0)$ and $y = A x + w$ with
$w \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R)$. In this case the messages stay Gaussian, and the
sum–product rules collapse into **Gaussian belief propagation**:

$$
\mu_{y \to x}(x) \propto \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac12 (y - A x)^\top R^{-1} (y - A x)\Big),
$$

and the belief update is closed-form:

$$
x^* = P^* A^\top R^{-1} y + \cdots,
\qquad
P^{*} = \left(P_0^{-1} + A^\top R^{-1} A\right)^{-1}.
$$

<Callout type="note" title="Same equations, different names">
This is *precisely* the Kalman update, the ridge-regression solution, and the
Gaussian AMP update — just with different notation. When you see a Kalman filter
in a robotics textbook, you're watching belief propagation in disguise.
</Callout>

## From belief propagation to GNN layers

Now replace the fixed messages with *learnable* functions. A GNN message from node
$v$ to node $u$ is:

$$
m_{v \to u}^{(t)} = \operatorname{MSG}_{\theta}\!\left(h_v^{(t-1)}, h_u^{(t-1)}, e_{vu}\right),
\qquad
h_u^{(t)} = \operatorname{UPD}_{\phi}\!\left(h_u^{(t-1)}, \sum_{v \in \mathcal{N}(u)} m_{v \to u}^{(t)}\right).
$$

The aggregation $\sum_{v \in \mathcal{N}(u)}$ is a sum-product over a local factor;
the readout is a global factor; the whole thing is **belief propagation with
trained potentials**. Below is the full mapping.

## Chemistry interlude: molecules are already graphs

Drug discovery is a natural graph problem — atoms are nodes, bonds are edges. And
because molecules are drawn *chemically*, it helps to write them chemically too.
For example, sulfonation of benzene:

$$
\ce{C6H6 + H2SO4 ->[\Delta] C6H5SO3H + H2O}
$$

The $\ce{}$ macro comes from the `mhchem` extension of KaTeX — the same extension
that renders equilibrium arrows, oxidation states, and units:

$$
\ce{CH3COO- + H2O <=> CH3COOH + OH-}
$$

For property prediction we don't need the drawing, just the connectivity. A GNN
reads a SMILES string like `CC(=O)Oc1ccccc1C(=O)O`, builds the bond graph, and
predicts logP — which is exactly what the notebook at the end of this article does.

## What Approximate Message Passing adds

Belief propagation is exact on trees but intractable when the factor $f$ couples
$O(N)$ variables — think compressed sensing with $y = Ax + w$ and dense $A$. AMP
sidesteps this by approximating the messages with a **Gaussian state per variable**:

$$
x^{(t+1)} = \eta\!\left(A^\top r^{(t)} + x^{(t)}; \; \theta^{(t)}\right)
$$

with the residual updated by the famous **Onsager correction**:

$$
r^{(t)} = y - A x^{(t)} + \frac{b^{(t)}}{\delta}\, r^{(t-1)}.
$$

The correction is what makes AMP match the replica-predicted MSE — no other iterative
solver gets this for free. And crucially, $\eta(\cdot)$ can be a *learned*
soft-thresholding network — giving a GAMP-style network that we build in the
`GAMP-GNN` project.

<Callout type="warning" title="Hand-wavy? Don't be">
The Onsager term is subtle. If you are implementing AMP from scratch, derive it
from the *state evolution* recursion before adding learnable parameters — the blog
companion to the `gamp-gnn` project page shows the full derivation.
</Callout>

## Summary

| Concept | Factor-graph name | GNN name | AMP name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local update | $\mu_{f \to x}$ | $\operatorname{MSG}_\theta$ | $\eta(\cdot)$ |
| Combine | $\prod \mu$ | sum aggregation | Gaussian state |
| Output | belief $b(x)$ | readout $R(\cdot)$ | estimator $\hat{x}$ |

Three fields, one skeleton. Learn it once — the derivations transfer everywhere.
